Five questions on that unit. Answers below — cover them while you work through.

1. Which set of responsibilities always stays with you, regardless of cloud service model?

A. Physical datacenter security and network hardware
B. Host operating system and runtime patching
C. Data, identities and access, endpoints, and configuration choices
D. The application stack and its underlying infrastructure

2. A workload moves from IaaS to PaaS. What changes for the operating system?

A. You remain fully responsible for it
B. The provider takes responsibility for it
C. Responsibility is split evenly
D. The OS layer no longer exists

3. A storage account in your tenant is misconfigured and exposes customer records publicly. Who is accountable?

A. The cloud provider, since it hosts the storage
B. You, because configuration choices are always yours
C. Neither — the platform worked as designed
D. It depends whether the provider published a security baseline

4. The AI shared responsibility model splits an AI-enabled application into three layers. Which layer covers the prompts people write and the acceptable use policies you set?

A. AI platform
B. AI application
C. AI usage
D. AI infrastructure

5. An attacker hides instructions inside a document your AI assistant reads, manipulating its output. What is this, and who mitigates it?

A. Prompt injection — your responsibility
B. Prompt injection — the platform provider’s responsibility
C. Data exfiltration — the platform provider’s responsibility
D. Model poisoning — shared by contract

Answers

1 — C. Four things never transfer: your data (classifying it, protecting it, governing it), identities and access, endpoints, and the configuration choices inside your tenant. Everything else shifts by service model.

2 — B. Under IaaS you secure the OS. Moving to PaaS hands the operating system and the runtime environment to the provider, leaving you application code, its configuration, access controls, and data.

3 — B. Configuration choices — service settings, resource permissions, network policies — are always the customer’s. A misconfigured storage account or overly permissive policy exposes data even when the platform underneath is secured properly.

4 — C. AI platform is the infrastructure, model, and platform-level safety controls. AI application is the app you build or deploy, its configuration, data sources, connectors, and plugins. AI usage is how people actually use it: their inputs, your policies, your oversight.

5 — A. Prompt injection embeds instructions in user input or external data to manipulate the AI’s behavior or outputs. It sits on the customer’s side, addressed through input validation, access controls, and monitoring.

The one most likely to catch you on the real exam is #3 — “the provider secures the platform” feels like it should cover storage, but configuration is always yours.
